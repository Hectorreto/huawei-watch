# Órbita (circular arena game)

`apps/orbit/` (bundle `com.hectorreto.orbita`, "Órbita" on the watch): a ball crosses a round arena the size of the screen; holding a half of the screen turns the arena, and with it the spikes and the diamond, but not the ball. Diamonds score, spikes end the game. An original game with the mechanic of the mobile game "360 Degree" (seen on its store pages in November 2026); none of its name, art or text is used.

## How it plays

- **Start:** the game opens paused, with the ball still in the centre; the first touch starts it. There is no pause button. After a loss the ball turns red where it hit, the line shows the record, and touches are ignored for 0.7 s (`OVER_PAUSE_MS`), since the player is usually still holding; the next touch starts a new game from 0.
- **Controls:** a full-screen `div` on top takes every touch; the left half (x < 233) turns the arena anticlockwise, the right half clockwise. The touch events carry `globalX` on the event itself (see `create-and-preview-the-app.md`, **Gestures**). The request was "hold to turn", but **lite has no press or release event for a finger that does not move** (checked in the Previewer, November 2026, with counters on screen; the simulator's event names are only `click`, `longpress`, `swipe` and `touch`):
  - `touchstart`, `touchmove` and `touchend` come only once the pointer has moved a few px: they are drag events. A still press fires none of them.
  - `longpress` comes while a still press is held, but then nothing comes when it is released (no `touchend`, no `click`), so it cannot start a turn that has to stop on release: a first try kept turning the arena forever.
  - `click` comes on releasing a short still press.
  So a held finger that moves even a little turns the arena at `TURN_SPEED` (3.2 rad/s) until it lifts (`touchend`), and a still tap or long press turns it one step (`TAP_TURN`, 0.4 rad, about 23 degrees) at `TAP_TURN_SPEED` (4 rad/s); a drag cancels the steps left. On the GT 6 Pro (November 2026) holding a finger still only turned one step, so the touch controls are a fallback and the crown is the main control.
- **Crown:** turning the crown turns the arena (the user's idea), in the crown's direction, and feels continuous on the GT 6 Pro. Lite gives a page no crown event before API 24 (`setMonitorForCrownEvents`, see `create-and-preview-the-app.md`, **Crown**), but a `slider` that has the crown focus (`rotation({ focus: true })`, as the clicker's lists, requested 50 ms after loading) changes its value as the crown turns and fires `change` with `value`. So a 100 px `slider` (`ref="crown"`, 0-1000) sits under the ring, hidden by it, and each change turns the arena by the difference times `CROWN_GAIN` (0.07 rad), at most `CROWN_MAX_STEP` (0.15 rad, less than a spike's width at the wall, so a spike cannot jump over the ball between two checks), straight onto the arena with no animation. When the value leaves 200-800 it is bound back to 500 or 501 (alternating, so the binding always changes). On the GT 6 Pro each crown event moved the value by about 2 (a test showed `d-2` after 267 events), so 0.02 rad per unit turned too little; 0.06 felt right on the watch (November 2026), and the user then asked for it a little faster: 0.07. In the Previewer the mouse wheel over the watch is the crown: one event of 10 per wheel gesture, however long. The crown does nothing while paused; the first tap starts the game.
- **Ball:** moves at a steady 225 px/s (`BALL_SPEED`), whatever the score. It started at 150 px/s plus 2 per point and was raised step by step up to 380 at the user's request, then made steady, brought down a lot to 200 and raised a little to 225, its heading turning slowly (`curve`, 0.04-0.1 rad/s to a random side, about 5 degrees over a crossing) so it bends a little. On the wall (inner edge 224 px from the centre) it heads back towards the centre, 1-5 degrees off to a random side (`BOUNCE_OFF_MIN`, `BOUNCE_OFF_MAX`), with a new curve. At first it went back the way it came, 5-20 and then 3-11 degrees off, with a curve of 0.1-0.3 rad/s; on the watch the bounces drifted too far from the centre, so the user asked for this.
- **Spikes:** always 10, hidden until the first bounce. Each bounce first checks the spikes where it hit (that is the loss), then moves them all into 2-5 groups (`MIN_GROUPS`, `MAX_GROUPS`, about equally often) of 1-5 spikes (`MAX_GROUP_SIZE`; with 2 groups always 5 and 5), handed out at random, each group at a random place on the wall, anywhere, at least `GROUP_SPACE` (0.3 rad) from the next group when 20 tries find room (checked in Node over 5,000 bounces). The new spikes are hidden and then shown 3 a frame (`REVEALS_PER_FRAME`), and only shown spikes count. Since a group may land right on the ball, the ball is immune to spikes for `IMMUNE_TIME` (0.35 s) after each bounce and shows light blue meanwhile; by then it is well away from the wall. (At first the groups were 2 or 3 at set places away from the hit, which never needed immunity; the user asked for random places and counts.) A spike touches the ball within 18 px of its centre; this is checked every 6 px of travel, also while the arena turns, so turning a spike into the ball loses too.
- **Diamond:** one at a time, at a random spot 40-170 px from the centre and at least 90 px from the ball; it turns with the arena. Touching it scores 1, vibrates briefly and places a new one.
- **Ruby:** a small red diamond (`common/ruby.png`, 20 px) that shows up for 8 s, first after 20 s of play and then 15-30 s after the last one went away (`EXTRA_FIRST`, `EXTRA_GAP_MIN`, `EXTRA_GAP_MAX`, `EXTRA_LIFE`), anywhere inside the arena away from the ball and the diamond, turning with it. It adds half the score, rounded down, at least 1 (`RUBY_SHARE`): 1 at 0-3 points, 5 at 10, 10 at 20.
- **Shield:** a green wheel with spikes (`common/shield.png`, 28 px) that shows up for 9 s, first after 15 s of play and then 10-20 s after (30 and 25-45 at first; the user asked for it more often), but not while the ball already has a shield. Touching it makes the ball green: the next spike hit takes the shield instead of the game, with the same moment of immunity (and light blue) as after a bounce. Neither extra is saved: closing the app drops them and the shield.
- **Score:** a large dim number in the centre, under everything that moves; the record shows under it while paused.

## Drawing

No canvas: a moving scene would need a full redraw every frame, a lite canvas takes only about 1,100 `fillRect` and has no `clearRect` (`compare-drawing-methods.md`). Everything is a fixed component moved with a bound `style`, as in the maze's gems:

- The arena is one `div` with a 9 px border and `border-radius` 233.
- The ball is a 20 px round `div` with bound `left`, `top` and `background-color`.
- The diamond is an `image` of `common/diamond.png` (30 px); the ruby and the shield are two more fixed `image`s, at -100 px when not showing.
- The 10 spikes are 10 fixed `image`s (no `for`, which closed the lab when its array was swapped), each with bound `src`, `left` and `top`. They are only for show: a hit is worked out from each spike's exact angle (`touchesSpike`). Lite cannot rotate an image, so `common/spike_00.png` ... `spike_23.png` hold one 32 px spike per 15 degrees (`SPIKE_DIRS`), pointing inwards, and each spike shows the one nearest its angle. Its `src` changes only when that changes, and while the arena turns at most `SRC_SWAPS_PER_FRAME` (2) spikes change it in a frame, in turn, since an `src` change is slow on the watch (the lab's i1). After a bounce the new spikes appear 3 a frame (`REVEALS_PER_FRAME`, about 4 frames) instead of all 10 changing in one frame, which the user felt as a hitch. Hidden spikes sit at -100 px.
- Tried and dropped (November 2026): one image per group of 1-5 spikes, curved along the wall, for each 15 degrees (120 images of 36-132 px), to move 5 images instead of 10. On the GT 6 Pro it averaged 33 ms a frame with a slowest frame of 132 ms, but a whole group snapping 15 degrees at a time looked odd while turning, and the lite build stores each PNG as a raw bitmap, so the HAP grew from 270 KB to 4.2 MB. After removing images, delete `entry/build`: the incremental build kept the old bitmaps in the HAP.
- The sprites are made by `tools/make_orbit_sprites.js` (DevEco's Node, no packages); the launcher icon by `tools/make_app_icons.py`.

A frame (`setInterval`, 33 ms) moves the ball by the real time since the last one (at most 0.1 s, so a slow frame slows the game rather than jumping) and assigns only bindings whose value changed, comparing with module arrays rather than reading the page's properties back: the ball's two coordinates every frame, the diamond and the spikes' positions only while the arena turns, about 24 values a frame then. On the drawing lab's numbers (one moving `div` 34 ms/frame; 100 bound `div`s 544 ms) that is expected to stay near 30 fps on the watch, but on the GT 6 Pro the game felt a little stuck (November 2026), with up to 10 spike `src` changes a frame while turning. Limiting the `src` changes helped, but the bounces still felt like a hitch, hence showing new spikes a few a frame. With `SHOW_FRAME_MS` on (it is now), the line under the score shows the average and the slowest ms per frame of the last second while playing, to measure it on the watch. In the Previewer it shows about 47 ms.

## Saving

`@system.storage` key `orbita` holds one comma-separated string: score, record, ball position, heading and curve, the arena's turn, how many spike groups there are (0 before the first bounce), the diamond, the 5 group sizes and the 5 group angles, and a format version, 2 (21 numbers). A version 1 save (10 spike angles) gives only the score and the record. It is written on each point, on each loss (already reset to a new game from 0, so a close right after losing starts fresh) and in `onDestroy`. On opening, the game is restored paused and the first touch carries on with the same score, ball and spikes. A save of another length is ignored. The screen is kept on (`brightness.setKeepScreenOn`) only while playing; paused, the system's timeout applies, which closes the app (see `compare-drawing-methods.md`, **Keep the screen on**), and the save restores it.

## Status

November 2026: checked in Node (the page's own code with stubbed storage, timers and clock; 200 games with random holds and taps: the ball stays inside, the score and record grow, the save restores) and it compiles with hvigor. With its debug profile (`orbita.p7b`, app ID `Orbita`, created November 2026) it builds a signed HAP of about 260 KB. In the Previewer: the first tap starts the game, a drag held on a half turns the arena that way and stops on release, taps turn it a step each, spikes appear after the first bounce pointing inwards and move on each bounce, diamonds score, losing shows the record and the next tap starts again, and the 90 px score shows large. On the GT 6 Pro (November 2026) it looks very good, a still finger turns only one step, and the crown turns the arena continuously and at a good speed (gain 0.06). Still to check there: the frame rate while turning (22 bindings a frame), and that closing and reopening resumes.
